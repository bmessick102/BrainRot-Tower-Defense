# Italian Brainrot Tower Defense (ibtd)

A single-player base-builder tower defense for the browser (desktop, and phones / tablets by touch). You mine gold (Bitcoin), build and upgrade defenses on an isometric grid, and survive the enemy waves for as long as you can. A wave only comes when you press Start Wave, by day or by night (the day/night cycle runs on its own), and every wave is tougher than the last. Research perks for every defense and economy building in the RV Lab, and see [CHANGELOG.md](CHANGELOG.md) for what's new in each version. For a one-document overview of the project (for presentations), see [docs/PROJECT_OVERVIEW.md](docs/PROJECT_OVERVIEW.md).

Built from [docs/BUILD_CHARTER.md](docs/BUILD_CHARTER.md) with JavaScript (ES modules), Phaser 3.90, and Vite. Players log in with email and password; their villages, achievements and the leaderboard are stored in [Supabase](https://supabase.com) (see [Online features](#online-features-accounts-villages-achievements-leaderboard)). Without a Supabase project set up, the game offers offline play with the save in the browser's `localStorage`.

## Running it

```bash
npm install        # once
npm run dev        # dev server with hot reload: open the URL it prints (…/BrainRot-Tower-Defense/)
npm test           # Vitest logic tests
npm run build      # static site in dist/ (GitHub Pages)
npm run preview    # serve dist/ locally
npm run prepare-art   # after adding or changing art in art-source/
```

**Controls:** left-click to select, collect, place, and use buttons (a mine showing its coin is collected without opening its panel; click it again, or any time the coin isn't showing, to open the panel) · drag the map to pan · scroll wheel to zoom · Esc to cancel placement, leave edit mode, or pause.

**Shop info:** every shop card has an ⓘ button that opens a popup with that building's cost, unlock level and how many you can build, its description, and its Level 1 stat bars. Clicking the card itself still buys it.

**Shop badge:** the red number on the Shop icon is how many more buildings and wall segments your Town Hall level allows. It's hidden at 0.

**Profile badge:** the red number on the profile pill (top left, when logged in) counts achievement rewards waiting to be claimed plus news you haven't seen yet: achievements earned and league promotions ([src/net/profileNews.js](src/net/profileNews.js)). Opening the Profile marks the news as seen (shown there with red NEW! tags); unclaimed rewards stay counted until claimed. "Seen" is remembered per account on each device.

**Walls:** buy them in the shop's Walls tab. Each wall is a single tile (`walls.segmentLength` 1), so they fit snugly around buildings: click to place, and you stay in wall-placing mode for the next one until you press Esc or run out of gold or walls. Every two walls side by side join into one connected fence, whether in a line, at a corner, at a T or running alongside each other. Walls level up like buildings and move in edit mode. **Upgrading many walls at once:** click a wall, then "Select connected walls (N)" to select every wall of the same level joined to it (all outlined; `wallGroup()` in [src/logic/buildings.js](src/logic/buildings.js)). One "Upgrade N walls · cost" button upgrades them all. Short on gold, it upgrades as many as you can afford, nearest to the clicked wall first ("Upgrade 6 of 26"), and shows what all of them would cost. "Select one wall" goes back; selling stays one wall at a time. Walls used to be 3-tile segments: a wall now costs a third of an old segment (17 gold at Level 1, the same per tile), keeps a segment's HP, and 3x as many are allowed (24 at Village Hall 1 up to 132). Older saves are converted automatically (save version 3): each old segment becomes three walls on the same tiles, at the same level. **Eight wall levels**, one picture each, unlocking at Village Hall 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 8, 9 and 10 (`townHall` on each `walls.levels` entry): 400 / 550 / 800 / 1,150 / 1,600 / 2,200 / 2,980 / 3,800 HP. They're cheap on purpose (10 / 25 / 60 / 150 / 350 / 800 / 1,800 / 4,000 gold), so a village that took a beating can wall up again quickly. Walls used to have 10 levels (a picture per 2); save version 4 moves old walls to Level ceil(L/2), so each keeps its picture (`migrateV3()` in [src/logic/save.js](src/logic/save.js)).

**Wall art:** one pillar per level, `bld_wall_lv1`–`lv8`: a rope-bound wooden post, a steel-banded post, a stone block, a riveted stone block, a gold-trimmed block, a spiked skull fortress block, the same block glowing with lava, and a lava tower. All eight are cropped to one shared box (`trim: group`), so every level keeps the same scale and baseline. Walls are drawn as one **connected wall**: each wall tile has its pillar on its center. Wherever two walls are side by side (lines, corners, T-junctions, walls running alongside each other; not diagonally), connecting pillars stand evenly between them, so neighboring pillars always overlap into a continuous wall. Thin wooden posts get 2-3 connectors and thick stone blocks 1, so pillars never stand further apart than 65% of their width (`wallLinksFor()`). Each wall draws its own pillar plus the connectors leading forward from it, layered back to front. `npm run prepare-art` measures each pillar's width and base into the generated `src/wallArtMeta.js`; see `wallParts()` / `placeWallPillar()` in [src/objects/WallView.js](src/objects/WallView.js), where `PILLAR_WIDTH` sets the on-screen size. Walls grow with every level, so a wall's level reads at a glance: 30% of a tile wide at Level 1 (a thin post) up to 58% at Level 8 (a big tower), in equal steps, about the same average size as before (more connecting pieces fill in small ones so they stay gap-free) (`wallPillarWidth()`). Upgrading a wall gives the same bounce as a building. The shop card, info panel, placement ghost and swap preview all show the level's pillar. Destroyed walls show a rubble pile on their tile: broken wooden posts with rope (`bld_wall_rubble_wood`, level 1), broken stone blocks (`bld_wall_rubble_stone`, levels 2-5, since level 2's post stands on a stone base), skull rubble (`bld_wall_rubble_skull`, level 6) or lava rubble (`bld_wall_rubble_lava`, levels 7-8), set in `WALL_RUBBLE` in BuildingView.js. The earlier 3-tile fence pictures are archived in `docs/archive/`, with the original (uncropped, 1254 px) level 6, 7 and 8 pictures; `art-source/buildings/bld_wall_lv6.png` / `lv7.png` / `lv8.png` are those pictures placed on the same 1024 canvas as the others, at level 5's width and baseline, so the shared crop still works.

**Moving buildings (the layout editor):** only in edit mode. Click the Edit Base Layout button (next to Shop). Edit mode works on a **draft layout** ([src/logic/layout.js](src/logic/layout.js)): the village itself doesn't change until you press **Save**, so storages keep their capacity, mines keep mining and nothing becomes walkable while buildings are picked up.
  - **Pick up:** click a building to carry it, then click a green spot to drop it. Drop it on top of one other building and the two swap places: the ghost snaps onto that building, which dims, and a ghost of it shows where it will go. The swap only goes green if both fit (`planSwap` in [src/logic/buildings.js](src/logic/buildings.js)).
  - **Hotbar:** the panel along the bottom. Click it while carrying a building to store it there; **Remove All** stores every building (Village Hall and walls included). Stored buildings are grouped into cards by type and level with a count badge (pages with arrows when there are many); click a card to carry the next one and drop it on the map. Walls keep coming from the same stack in a row until it runs out (Esc to stop). Buildings from the hotbar never swap.
  - **Shift:** the round pad above the hotbar (left) slides every placed building one tile at a time along the map's grid lines (`shiftLayout` in layout.js); buildings in the hotbar stay there. Everything moves together, so nothing can overlap; a shift that would push anything off the grass is refused with a banner. Like everything else in edit mode it only sticks once you Save.
  - **Save** (only once the hotbar is empty; it shows how many are left) moves every building to its new spot at once, saves the game and shows "Layout saved!".
  - **Cancel**, Esc, right click or the Edit button leaves. With changes made it asks first ("Leave without saving? ... N buildings are still in your hotbar"), and leaving puts everything back exactly where it was. Esc while carrying a building just puts it back (or back in the hotbar).
  - While editing, the shop and Start Wave are unavailable and the gnomes are hidden. Edit mode isn't available during a wave or the tutorial.

**Deploying:** pushing to `main` runs [.github/workflows/deploy.yml](.github/workflows/deploy.yml). It runs the tests, builds, and publishes `dist/` to GitHub Pages. `vite.config.js` sets `base: '/BrainRot-Tower-Defense/'` to match the repo name ([github.com/bmessick102/BrainRot-Tower-Defense](https://github.com/bmessick102/BrainRot-Tower-Defense)); the game is at https://bmessick102.github.io/BrainRot-Tower-Defense/.

In [src/mapTiles.js](src/mapTiles.js), `imageWidth` / `imageHeight` are that drawn size and `originX` / `originY` (1186, 134) is the top corner of tile (0, 0) in world pixels. ## Days, nights, and waves

The day/night clock ([src/logic/dayNight.js](src/logic/dayNight.js)) is a cycle of its own, about 8 minutes long (`CONFIG.dayNight`), separate from the waves. It runs on game time (paused with the game) once the tutorial reaches its first wave:

| Phase | Length | What it looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day | `daySeconds` (4 min) | Full daylight. |
| Dusk | `duskSeconds` (30 s) | The dusk map fades in over the day map, then the night map fades in over it (once it counts as dark). Sprites warm up to a peach tint, then go moonlit. |
| Night | `nightSeconds` (3 min) | Dark: building light pools and glowing enemies. |
| Dawn | `dawnSeconds` (30 s) | The day map fades back. |

**Waves come when you press Start Wave** (beside the wave pill at the top of the HUD), at whatever time it is: start one by day and they attack by day, start one at night and they attack in the dark. The clock keeps running through the fight, and beating a wave doesn't change the time. **Restless nights** ([src/logic/enrage.js](src/logic/enrage.js), `CONFIG.rules.night`): while it's dark (`isDark()` in [src/logic/clockLight.js](src/logic/clockLight.js), from about a third of the way into dusk to two thirds into dawn), a wave's enemies are restless: +25% damage (`damage` 1.25) and +15% speed (`speed` 1.15), on top of any enrage, with a light red flush (`RESTLESS_TINT` in EnemyView.js). In exchange every kill pays +25% gold and research points (`lootBonus`, `nightLootMult()`), and so do the wave-cleared research points when the wave is beaten in the dark. It's checked live, so a wave started late in the day turns restless when night falls (banner "Night: the enemies are restless! +25% gold & RP", the wave pill adds "Restless") and calms down at daylight (`restlessChanged` event). In the dark the Start Wave button says "Night: restless, +25% loot". Otherwise the time of day changes nothing: gnomes work and mines produce day and night. Pressing Start Wave: the banner says `Wave 3 incoming!`, the wave starts at once (the attack music cuts in, a pulsing red "!" appears over each entrance enemies come from), and the Retry checkpoint is saved just before it. During a wave, the shop, edit mode, upgrades, selling and collecting from mines are locked (mines keep filling). When it's beaten: the cleared banner, every destroyed building is rebuilt and damaged ones heal for free, autosave, and Start Wave works again right away.

**Wave stars** ([src/logic/waveStars.js](src/logic/waveStars.js), `CONFIG.rules.waveStars`): every beaten wave is rated by how many of the buildings standing when it started (walls don't count) were destroyed during it (`wave.startBuildings`, `wave.lost`, counted in `damageBuilding`). None lost: 3 stars. At least 75% still standing (`twoStarShare`): 2 stars. Otherwise 1. 3 stars pays +50% of the wave's gold bounty and research points on top (`bonus`), 2 stars +20%. The wave pill shows the rating it's on track for (`Wave 7 · 23 enemies left ★★☆`), and the cleared banner shows the result. Stars count toward the Star Collector achievement (100 stars) and the profile's "Wave stars" stat. The free rebuild after the wave happens either way.

**First-time tips** ([src/logic/tips.js](src/logic/tips.js)): after the first tutorial, the guide explains each system the first time it shows up, in the same bubble as the tutorial, with "Got it" and "No more tips". The tips cover research points (the first RP earned), wave stars (the first wave beaten), special waves and Da Baby Car (when one is next), restless nights (the first night attack) and edit mode (the first time it's opened). Tips that come up together wait in a queue; seen tips are saved per village (`tips`). Similarly, the research window hides tiers beyond the RV Lab's level ("? ? ? · Locked · RV Lab lv 3") so new players see a few options at a time.

**HUD clock:** left of the RP pill under the gold bar, a sun (day, dawn) or moon (dusk, night) with the phase and the time left in it, e.g. `Day 3:12` (`refreshClock()` in [src/scenes/UIScene.js](src/scenes/UIScene.js)). Hidden until the clock runs.

- **Wave numbers** count up in the order you fight them: the next wave is waves cleared + 1, whatever the Village Hall level. Losing a wave and retrying keeps its number.
- **How hard a wave is** ([src/logic/waves.js](src/logic/waves.js) `scaledWaveComposition`): the **Village Hall level picks the base wave** (which enemy types appear, how many, from how many corners): Village Hall 1–4 use `config.waves[0..3]`, Village Hall 5–10 the growth formula (`rules.endlessCountGrowth` / `endlessHpGrowth`). Then **every wave survived ramps it up** (`rules.waveRamp`, linear): +8% enemies (`countPerWave`) and +5% enemy HP (`hpPerWave`) per earlier wave, so wave 11 has 1.8x the base enemies at 1.5x HP. The ramp never resets, so waves keep getting harder even if you never upgrade, and upgrading the Village Hall jumps to a bigger base wave with new enemy types. Enemy types the hall's base wave doesn't have never appear (no flyers before you can build anti-air).
- **Crowd cap** (`CONFIG.rules.crowdCap`, `applyCrowdCap()` in [src/logic/waves.js](src/logic/waves.js)): a wave never sends more than 400 enemies (bosses not counted). A bigger one keeps the same mix at about 400, and every enemy in it is "packed" by the wave's pack factor (its old total enemy health / the new one; e.g. ×4.55 at wave 128, which would otherwise send ~1,800). A packed enemy has that many times the health, damage (hits, shots, Wall Breaker blasts) and gold / RP, Neegies dropped by a packed balloon are packed too, and packed enemies come that much further apart (`spawnInterval × pack`). So the wave is as strong per second and pays as much, with far fewer sprites to draw and move. Packed enemies are drawn a little bigger (+12% per doubling, up to +35%, `maxSizeBoost`). Da Baby Car is never packed.
- **Special waves** ([src/logic/waves.js](src/logic/waves.js), `CONFIG.specialWaves`): every 3rd wave (`every`) is a special one instead of a normal wave, rotating through `order` and skipping types whose enemies (`needs`) aren't in that Village Hall's base wave yet, so there's always a counter:

  | Type | Enemies | From |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Swarm | Grunts and Bitcoin Stealers, lots of them | Village Hall 2 |
  | Air Raid | Bombers and Neegy Balloons only (70% strength) | Village Hall 3 |
  | Ground Assault | Every ground enemy of the normal wave, no flyers | Village Hall 3 |
  | Heist | Mostly Bitcoin Stealers (60% strength) | Village Hall 2 |
  | Siege | Tanks and a few Wall Breakers | Village Hall 4 |

  A special wave has about the same strength as the normal wave it replaces: its total enemy HP (x the type's `strength`) is split over the type's `mix`, with the same HP multiplier and spawn corners. If that would be more than `maxEnemiesFactor` (2x) the normal wave's enemy count, there are fewer, tougher enemies instead. Clearing one pays `bonusBounty` (+50%) of the wave's bounty on top, shown in the cleared banner. **Warning:** all through the build time before it, the HUD pill turns red (`Next: Wave 6 · Air Raid!`) and the Start Wave button turns red, widens and shows the type's `hint` ("Flyers only: build anti-air"). During the wave the pill says `Wave 6 · Air Raid · 19 enemies left`, and the Village Hall's info says `Next attack: wave 6 (Air Raid)`.
- **Quick starts:** pressing Start Wave within `rules.quickStartSeconds` (10 s) of clearing the last wave counts toward the "Bring It On" achievement (5 quick starts).
- **The ruined Village Hall (level 0):** every new game starts with the hall in ruins (`CONFIG.townHall.ruin`, art `bld_townHall_lv0`). Nothing can be built, no gnomes live there, the clock and the Start Wave button wait, and the HUD says "Rebuild your Village Hall to begin". Rebuilding it to level 1 costs 100 (`townHall.levels[0].upgradeCost`); starting gold is 250, so the first Mine (150) is still affordable. Tutorial step 1 is "This is your Village Hall, but it lies in ruins! Click it and rebuild it to Level 1", and the guide points at the hall, then at its Rebuild button. Skip tutorial is offered on steps 1-2: skipping with the hall still in ruins gives the starter base and exactly 100 gold to rebuild it; skipping after rebuilding keeps the hall and your gold.
- **Tutorial:** the clock stays at day until the Cannon is placed. Then wave 1 comes right away (no button needed) and the clock starts running; the last step explains Start Wave and the Village Hall upgrade. Skipping the tutorial starts at day, waiting for Start Wave.
- **Stalled waves enrage** ([src/logic/enrage.js](src/logic/enrage.js), `rules.enrage`): 90 s after a wave's last enemy spawns, the banner says "The enemies are enraged!", the HUD adds "Enraged!", and the remaining enemies glow red. Their damage (melee hits and ranged shots) climbs by 1x and their speed by 0.5x every 30 s, up to 5x damage and 2x speed, so no wave can last forever.
- **Losing a wave:** Retry rewinds to the base (and the time of day) as that wave began, so you can rebuild and press Start Wave when ready (same wave number).
- **Saves** store the clock, and a loaded game carries on from its time of day. A save is never made mid-wave, so that wave comes again when you press Start Wave.
- **Visuals** ([src/objects/DayNight.js](src/objects/DayNight.js), [src/objects/BuildingView.js](src/objects/BuildingView.js)): dusk / night / dawn map crossfades (`skyBlend()` in [src/logic/clockLight.js](src/logic/clockLight.js): dawn is dusk run backwards through its own picture, and the night map starts exactly when it counts as dark), a warm twilight tint then a moonlit tint, and colored light pools around buildings. Each building is lit by its own pool: instead of the moonlit tint, it gets a gradient tint that is warm and bright at the base (white blended 20% toward its pool color, `BUILDING_LIT_WARMTH`) and soft moonlight at the top (`BUILDING_LIT_TOP`), fading in with the pools. Walls and rubble keep the moonlit tint. The light pools and enemy glows only show once it's dark (`DARK_AT` in dayNight.js: past about a third of the way into dusk), rising to full strength at night (`darkness()` in DayNight.js); by day nothing glows. The tint and light settings are constants at the top of those files.

Every defense (traps and the F.L.O.C.K Tower included) has three ability paths, A, B and C, of five tiers each, like Bloons TD. The trees, costs and level steps are in [src/abilityConfig.js](src/abilityConfig.js).

**How players get them:**
1. **Build the RV Lab** (Economy tab of the shop): 2x2, 1 allowed from Village Hall 4, 5 levels (4,000 / 10,000 / 25,000 / 60,000 / 90,000 gold). Level N needs Village Hall N+3 (`townHall` on each level in `CONFIG.rvlab`; checked in `checkUpgrade`).
2. **Research** in the RV Lab (its Research button, or any defense's Abilities button, which opens the same window on that defense): **one pick per tier** for each defense type: Path A's, B's or C's option, in any mix (A1, B2, A3...). Research costs research points: 80 / 240 / 600 / 1,400 / 3,000 RP per tier, never refunded. A destroyed RV Lab can't research until it's rebuilt; research already done stays.

## Walls and pathfinding

- Ground enemies ([src/logic/pathfinding.js](src/logic/pathfinding.js)) pick their target as before (the nearest building; **walls are never a target**). Then they follow the cheapest route across the tile grid. Open ground costs 1 per tile. A standing wall adds `maxHP × pathing.wallCostPerHp`, and another building adds `maxHP × pathing.buildingCostPerHp + pathing.buildingExtraCost`. So enemies walk around a wall when the detour is cheaper and smash through when the base is sealed or the detour is long. Higher-level walls are worth longer detours.
- When the cheapest route goes through a wall, the enemy walks up to it and attacks it until it breaks. Destroyed walls and buildings are rubble that can be walked over. Everything destroyed is rebuilt for free once the wave is beaten (see Villager Houses and ruins below).
- **Spawning:** enemies appear at the map edge where each dirt path runs off-screen (`MAP.edgeSpawnPoints` in mapTiles.js). Ground troops march down the path to its entrance (`MAP.spawnPoints`, where the "!" markers are), then pathfind from there. Bombers fly straight in. The longer walk adds a few seconds before each wave reaches the base. Enemies arrive spread out, up to 2 tiles from the corner (`SPAWN_JITTER`), and ground troops funnel down to 0.75 tiles as they reach the path entrance (`ENTRY_SPREAD`). Each wave picks its corners at random from all 4: Waves 1–2 use 1 corner (Wave 1 is always the top, for the tutorial), Waves 3–6 use 2, Waves 7–8 use 3, and Wave 9 onward uses all 4.
- **Corner fog:** soft fog puffs drift in each corner of the screen, day and night, under the HUD (`buildFog()` and `FOG` in [src/scenes/UIScene.js](src/scenes/UIScene.js); the puff texture is `fx_fog`, drawn in BootScene).
- Bombers fly straight over walls.
- **Sneaker Shark** (`shark`, `jumpsWalls: true` in config): a fast raider (HP 180, speed 1.3, 18 damage) that **leaps over walls** instead of walking around or breaking them. It paths as if walls were open ground and hops in a quick arc while crossing one. Other buildings still block it, and every ground defense can hit it. It first appears in wave 3 (3 sharks, then 5 in wave 4; later waves grow by the endless formula).
- **Money Stealer** (`thief`, art `enemy_thief`): a fast, fragile gold raider (HP 60, speed 1.4, 10 base damage, bounty 8). It targets only Bitcoin Mines and Storages (`prefers` in config), whichever is nearest, and ignores everything else until all of them are destroyed. It hits those buildings 3x as hard (30 per hit, `preferredDamageMultiplier`), and since hits on a Storage drain gold, it also steals gold quickly. Against anything else it deals 0.4x (4 per hit, `otherDamageMultiplier`). It **hops over walls** like the Sneaker Shark (`jumpsWalls: true`), so walls don't stop it: defenses have to. It also **spawns invisible** (`invisibleUntilJump: true`): defenses can't aim at it (`isValidTarget` in [src/logic/combat.js](src/logic/combat.js)) until its first wall jump, or its first hit on a building if no wall is in the way (`revealEnemy`, event `enemyRevealed`). Splash and Diddypult pools aimed at other enemies still hurt it, and taking damage doesn't reveal it. While invisible it's drawn at about 38% opacity with a faint shimmer; when revealed it pops to full opacity with a white puff. How to Play shows an Invisible tag and "Hit by 5 of 6 defenses once revealed". Waves: 3 in wave 2, 5 in wave 3, 6 in wave 4, then the endless formula. Logic: `pickTarget()` / `enemyDamageVs()` in [src/logic/enemies.js](src/logic/enemies.js).

## Online features (accounts, villages, achievements, leaderboard)

**What players get:** a login screen (Log In / Sign Up tabs; sign-up asks for a username, email and password). A saved login skips the screen. The title screen shows who's signed in (with Log Out) and START GAME plays the current village. Its button row:
- **Villages:** up to 5 villages per account (`MAX_BASES`), each with its own progress, picture, wave and best wave. Play one, Rename one, make a New Village (you name it; starts from the tutorial), or Delete one (asks first; achievements and leaderboard rank stay). On the first login, a browser that still has a village saved from before accounts offers to **import** it (the original stays in the browser; a Villages button offers it later too).
- **Achievements:** 26 achievements in three tabs (Village, Battle, Builder), earned once per account across all its villages. Each pays a one-time gold reward, claimed from the Achievements window (in-game Profile, top left) into the village being played (it has to fit in storage, and not during a wave). The list is in [src/logic/achievements.js](src/logic/achievements.js); unlocking shows a toast in game with its own sound (`sfx_achievement.mp3`; the wave-clear sound stands in if the file is missing).
- **Leaderboard:** the top 10 players by furthest wave survived (one row per player, ties go to whoever got there first), with the player's own rank added if lower.
- **Leagues:** every player's furthest wave puts them in a league with its own badge: Bronze (wave 1+), Silver (4+), Gold (7+), Crystal (10+), Champion (15+), WarLord (25+) (`LEAGUES` in [src/logic/leagues.js](src/logic/leagues.js); art `ui_league_<key>.png` in art-source/ui). The badge shows on each leaderboard row, beside your name on the title screen, and the leaderboard footer says what the next league takes. Reaching a new league shows a "New league!" toast in game.
- **Village progress carries over:** a village's furthest wave is at least the number of waves it has cleared (`catchUpBestWave` in [src/net/session.js](src/net/session.js)), applied whenever a village is saved, at every login, and when a village is opened. Progress made offline, before tracking existed, or by editing the save still counts toward the league badge and the leaderboard. It only ever raises the number.

**How it fits together:**
- [src/net/session.js](src/net/session.js): the account while the game runs. `session.storage` is the Storage the game saves through (`setGameStorage` in GameState.js): with a village open, `ibtd.save` / `ibtd.checkpoint` read and write that village. Changes are kept in memory, mirrored to `localStorage` (`ibtd.cloud.<id>`) and uploaded 1.5 s later (Pause → Save uploads right away). A change that never reached the server (tab closed, offline) is recovered from the mirror at the next login. Stats, earned achievements and claims queue in `ibtd.outbox.<user>` the same way.
- [src/net/AchievementTracker.js](src/net/AchievementTracker.js): listens to game events (kills, traps, beams, waves, mines, upgrades), bumps counters, records the furthest wave / best Village Hall, and awards achievements.
- [src/net/supabaseBackend.js](src/net/supabaseBackend.js) talks to Supabase; [src/net/localBackend.js](src/net/localBackend.js) is a stand-in that keeps accounts in the browser. The dev server uses it while there are no keys (or with `?backend=local`), so everything can be tried without a server. It's never used in a build.
- Scenes / windows: [AuthScene](src/scenes/AuthScene.js) (login), [VillagesWindow](src/objects/VillagesWindow.js), [AchievementsWindow](src/objects/AchievementsWindow.js), [LeaderboardWindow](src/objects/LeaderboardWindow.js), [Modal](src/objects/Modal.js) (window + confirm / name prompts), [TextField](src/objects/TextField.js) (HTML text boxes over the canvas; `dom.createContainer` in main.js).

**Setting up Supabase (once):**
1. Create a free project at [supabase.com](https://supabase.com) (any name, e.g. "italian-brainrot-td"; pick a region near your players; save the database password somewhere).
2. **SQL Editor → New query:** paste all of [supabase/schema.sql](supabase/schema.sql) and press **Run**. It creates the tables, the leaderboard function, and the security rules (each player can only touch their own villages, stats and achievements; the leaderboard shows usernames only, never emails).
3. **Authentication → Sign In / Providers → Email:** keep Email enabled and **Confirm email** on (players get a link that brings them back to the game, logged in; sign-up never reveals whether an email already has an account). Set **Minimum password length** to 8 and **Password requirements** to letters and digits (the game checks the same rule).
   - **Emails need your own sender:** Supabase's built-in email only reaches your project's team members, a few per hour. Under **Authentication → Emails → SMTP Settings**, connect a provider (e.g. [Resend](https://resend.com), free tier).
   - **Authentication → URL Configuration:** Site URL `https://bmessick102.github.io/BrainRot-Tower-Defense/`; add `http://localhost:5173/BrainRot-Tower-Defense/` to Redirect URLs (for the dev server).
4. **Project Settings → API** (or the **Connect** button): copy the **Project URL** and the **anon / publishable** key into [src/backendConfig.js](src/backendConfig.js). Both are safe to publish: the security rules decide what they can do. Never paste the `service_role` / secret key.
5. **Robot check (CAPTCHA):** create a free [Cloudflare Turnstile](https://dash.cloudflare.com/?to=/:account/turnstile) widget for `bmessick102.github.io` and `localhost`. Put its **site key** in `TURNSTILE_SITE_KEY` (src/backendConfig.js) and push; then, in Supabase, **Authentication → Attack Protection → Enable CAPTCHA protection**, provider Turnstile, with its **secret key**. (In that order: once Supabase requires CAPTCHA, a game without the site key can't log in.)
6. Commit and push. The deployed game now requires a login.

**Security notes:** passwords go straight to Supabase over HTTPS and are stored only as bcrypt hashes. Row Level Security limits every player to their own villages, stats and achievements. The leaderboard shows usernames only. Because the game runs in the browser, the database also checks progress (`check_base` in schema.sql): a village's best wave can't pass its Village Hall level (10 max), and past wave 10 it rises only one wave per night the database itself counted, at most one night per 30 seconds of real time. A player's leaderboard wave can't pass what their villages back up, and an imported village counts for at most its Village Hall level. A determined cheater can still fake progress at a believable pace; only a server running the waves could stop that.

## Saving

Logged in, each village's save and checkpoint live in Supabase (`bases` table) with a copy on the device (see Online features); the keys below are what the game itself reads and writes, redirected into the open village. Offline, they're plain `localStorage` keys: `ibtd.save` (current game), `ibtd.checkpoint` (the base as dusk falls, before that night's wave; used by Retry), `ibtd.settings` (Music and Effects volume, 0-1; an older single `volume` seeds both). Saves never happen while enemies are on the field. The format is version 2 (adds the clock); version 1 saves are converted when loaded. In the browser console on the dev server, `window.__IBTD__` is the running Phaser game. It doesn't exist in production builds.

A shot that starts within a tile of its target (a balloon hovering over the tower that's shooting it) begins partway along so it still visibly reaches the body. Damage numbers pop up over the enemy's body too.
- **Gnome villagers** ([src/objects/Villagers.js](src/objects/Villagers.js), art `art-source/villagers/villager_gnome.png`): decoration only, so enemies ignore them. Gnomes come only from Villager Houses (none at the start); they spread out over the village by day. Each gnome has its own direction from the hall (evenly spaced, reshuffled each morning) and wanders 2.5–11 tiles out that way, picking spots away from where the other gnomes are and are headed. They follow tile paths around buildings (no cutting corners) and hop over walls with a little jump, though they never stop on one. A wall tile costs as much as 3 tiles of walking (`WALL_HOP_COST`), so they still walk around a short wall when that is about as quick. They ease apart when they get within 1.2 tiles, and step out of the door at slightly different places. When a wave starts they head inside (or out to repair), and when it's beaten they come back out one by one; the time of day doesn't matter. Tuning is in `VILLAGER_SETTINGS` at the top of that file.
- **Wizard fireball effects:** the fireball glows (bright outline, pulsing halo, white-hot core) and trails gold, orange, and purple sparks. It stays full-bright at night. On landing: a white-hot flash, a spark burst, rising embers, and a purple shockwave ring at the splash radius (`attachFireballFx` / `fireballImpact` in [src/objects/Effects.js](src/objects/Effects.js)).
- **Click effect:** clicking a building makes it squash and spring back while two gold rings ripple out from its footprint (`pop()` in BuildingView).
- **Walls and pathfinding** (charter §19 lists both as out of scope): see "Walls and pathfinding". Building limits gain `wall`: walls per Village Hall level, 24 at VH1 rising by 12 per level to 132 at VH10. The save format stores each wall's direction (`orient: 'x' | 'y'`).
- **Buildings may touch:** `rules.buildingGap` is 0 again (the 1-tile gap was removed).
- **Edit mode for moving** (charter §5 put Move in the info panel): buildings can only be moved in edit mode, opened with the Edit Base Layout button. The info panel has no Move button.
- Cole's Bitcoin Mine, Archer, Wizard, and arrow art turned out to have real transparency, so they're wired in instead of placeholders (charter §15 expected a painted checkerboard).
